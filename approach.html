Our approach

Timber first.
Then the drawing.

You don’t need a finished design. We’ll help you work out what your timber can become, and what’s worth keeping just as it is.

From your first thought to your home

A considered way
of making.

Tell us its story.

Show us the wood, tell us where it came from and describe the piece you have in mind. A few photographs and rough measurements help. So does knowing which marks you’d hate to lose.

You bring: the history, an idea and a few photographs.

Find the sound timber.

We look at what can safely be reused: the thickness, condition, old fixings and any movement. Some timber needs time to settle. Some needs a different idea. We’ll talk through both before committing to a design.

We consider: condition, usable dimensions and the right form.

Work out the details together.

We agree the proportions, joinery and finish, then put the design, cost and expected timing in writing. You can see how the piece will fit your room before the making begins.

You receive: a clear drawing, scope and written quotation.

Make it ready for everyday life.

We prepare, join and finish each part with the way you’ll use it in mind. When it’s ready, we plan the handover and explain the small habits that help the timber age well.

It comes home with: a care note and a record of its timber story.

Working with what you have

Every board has
its own ideas.

Oak, with its history intact.

Dense, characterful and often full of old nail marks. A former beam can become a generous table top, with the sound grain kept and the softer areas worked around.

Photographs suggest a finish and feeling; they don’t identify the timber species. We assess the actual material before recommending a design.

Before we begin

A few good
questions.

Can you work with timber I already own?

That’s the starting point we love. Share photographs of the whole piece or stack, close-ups of any damage and approximate measurements. We’ll discuss whether it can suit what you have in mind before arranging an assessment.

What if I don’t have enough wood?

We can explore a smaller piece or discuss pairing your timber with compatible reclaimed material. You’ll know which parts are yours and which are added before the design is agreed.

Will you keep the scratches and nail holes?

Where they’re sound, useful and part of what you love, yes. We’ll agree the balance between keeping character and creating a surface that’s comfortable and practical to use.

How much will my piece cost?

It depends on the condition of the timber, the design and the work needed to prepare it. Reusing material still takes care and time. We provide a written quotation once the scope is clear; there’s no price attached to sending an initial enquiry.

How long does a commission take?

We agree an estimated schedule after assessing the wood and design. Drying, repairs and the workshop’s current commitments all affect timing. Tell us early if you have a particular move or occasion in mind.

Can we visit the workshop?

Visits are by arrangement, so we can give you and your piece our full attention. Mention a visit in your enquiry and we can discuss the practical details.
